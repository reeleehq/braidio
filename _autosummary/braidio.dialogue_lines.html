braidio.dialogue_lines#

Addressable dialogue lines (braidio#145): one take, every turn re-voiceable.

render_dialogue_lines() is braidio.conversation.render_dialogue()’s one Text-to-Dialogue pass, timed: it returns a DialogueTake whose DialogueLines say where each turn sits in the take. rerender_line() re-synthesizes ONE turn and splices it in, leaving every other line’s audio (and its cross-turn prosody) untouched and unbilled. line_cost() quotes a line before it is rendered.

Three choices keep a take editable many times over:

  • A lossless master. Beside the deliverable (take.path, in the take’s output_format) sits a FLAC master (take.master). Splices operate on the master; the deliverable is re-encoded from it after each edit. So an unedited line is always ONE lossy generation from the master, however many edits came before, instead of one more generation per edit.

  • Content-checked takes. A take records the master’s SHA-256. A take whose master has since changed (another edit wrote over it in place) is refused with StaleTakeError, before any spend, rather than cut at offsets that no longer describe the file.

  • Cuts in the pauses, with fades. A re-rendered line replaces the take from the middle of the pause before it to the middle of the pause after it (the file’s edges for the first and last line), and brings the same amount of its own lead-in and decay along, so neither the old line’s tail nor the new line’s is lost. Each join gets mixing’s join fade, out and in.

A rendered exchange whose turns are addressable lines.

path is the deliverable (in output_format); master the lossless FLAC every splice operates on, and master_sha256 its content hash, so a take whose master has changed since is refused instead of mis-cut. lines are the turns in order, timed (turns is their (role, text) view); cast and output_format are what rerender_line() needs to re-voice one.

A take is checked when built: one line per turn, indexed 0..n-1 in order, each with its turn’s role and text, every role in the cast, and the lines in time order without overlap.

to_dict()/from_dict() round-trip it through JSON, so a host can store a take and re-render a line of it in a later request. A stored dict is data the host wrote: from_dict checks its consistency, NOT where path and master point. A host that keeps takes for several users must check both against its own root before trusting them, since rerender_line() writes to them.

braidio.dialogue_lines.render_dialogue_lines(turns, cast=ConversationCast(roles={'A': 'cgSgspJ2msm6clMCkdW9', 'B': 'iP95p4xoKVk53GoZ742B'}, model_id='eleven_v3', settings={'stability': 0.45}), *, out_path, api_key=None, output_format='mp3_44100_128', seed=None, cache=True, refresh=False, return_cache_status=False)[source]#

render_dialogue(), as addressable lines.

ONE Text-to-Dialogue pass (braidio.tts.text_to_dialogue_with_timestamps()), so the cross-turn prosody of render_dialogue is kept, and each turn’s place in the take comes from the API’s own voice segments (turns timed as overlapping are split at the overlap’s midpoint; see DialogueLine). Returns a DialogueTake; rerender_line() re-voices one of its lines.

out_path gets the API’s audio as is; beside it, the lossless master (MASTER_SUFFIX) later edits splice. Unlike render_dialogue there is no R-tight pass: trimming the dead gaps would move every line after the first trim away from the timings the API reported.

Billing, caching and the key are render_dialogue’s: the whole exchange’s characters, once; a cache hit (audio and timings, stored together) costs $0 and makes no API call, and return_cache_status=True returns (take, was_cached) to say which. api_key reaches the ElevenLabs client only — not the cache key, not the take. An empty turn, an unknown role or an unspliceable output_format is refused before any spend; a response lacking some turn’s timings raises DialogueTimingError naming them (re-roll with refresh=True).

Return type:

DialogueTake | tuple[DialogueTake, bool]

braidio.dialogue_lines.rerender_line(take, index, *, text=None, api_key=None, out_path=None, seed=None, cache=True, refresh=False, return_cache_status=False, synthesize=<function synthesize_line>)[source]#

Re-voice line index of take and splice it in; the others stay.

The line (text, default its current text — put eleven_v3 [audio tags] in it to change the delivery) is voiced ALONE by synthesize (default synthesize_line(): a single-input timed Text-to-Dialogue call with its role’s voice and the cast’s model and settings). Only its characters are billed (line_cost() quotes them first).

The take is cut from the middle of the pause before the line to the middle of the pause after it (the file’s edges for the first and last line), and the new voice goes in with as much of its own lead-in and decay as the cut removed, so the pauses keep their length and every later line moves by the change in the line’s duration. Joins are faded (mixing’s join fade). The splice runs on the lossless master; the deliverable is then encoded once from it in the take’s output_format.

Refused before any spend: empty text, a role the cast lacks, an unspliceable format, and a stale take (StaleTakeError: its master has changed since, typically because an in-place edit returned a newer take — use that one). A response without timings for the line raises DialogueTimingError; it is cached, so re-roll with refresh=True.

Cached like any dialogue call: re-rendering the same text with the same seed is a $0 cache hit, so pass refresh=True (or a new seed) to re-roll a line. return_cache_status=True returns (take, was_cached).

Written to out_path (default: over take.path, in place) with its master beside it. Returns the new DialogueTake; take itself is a value and is not changed, but after an in-place edit it is stale.

Return type:

DialogueTake | tuple[DialogueTake, bool]
