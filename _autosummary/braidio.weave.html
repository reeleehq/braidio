braidio.weave#

Weave narration + audio clips on a timeline (#21) — the reusable mix engine.

Two generic, Hamilton-agnostic primitives:

  • extract_padded() — extract [start, end] from a source asset but padded by pre/post-roll (so the words are captured cleanly) with in/out fades on the padded edges. The padded, faded edges are what we tuck under the neighbouring narration.

  • weave_timeline() — place ordered narration/clip parts on a timeline where each clip overlaps its neighbours by clip_edge_overlap_s and its faded padded edges duck under the speech, so speech stays dominant, then mix (amix sum) and loudness-normalize.

This is the audio counterpart of a video timeline; it consumes plain file paths

  • numbers, no Genius/lyrics knowledge (that resolution lives in graph/align.py and the Hamilton adapters). Ducking here is achieved by fade-shaped overlap; a dynamic sidechain duck (duck_db) is a documented refinement (see #21 / the research).

class braidio.weave.TurnOverlap(max_s=0.0, jitter=0.3, duck_db=0.0, seed=0)[source]#

Bases: object

How much the next speaker may start before the previous one finishes.

The pure, render-time view of WeaveConfig’s overlap_* knobs, so the layout and the mixer take one value instead of four loose floats. max_s == 0 means no interruption (the historical butt-join with a crossfade). It lives here, not in weave_config, so the weave stays free of the TTS/mixing imports that module carries.

jitter shortens each overlap by a seeded random fraction up to that amount (so it is not mechanical); duck_db (≤ 0) lowers the outgoing voice over the overlap; seed makes both reproducible — WeaveConfig passes its voice_seed, so one seed fixes the casting and the interruptions.

The overlap is measured between file boundaries: the layout sees each part’s duration only, so a beat that opens with its own lead silence (Narration.lead_gap_s) spends the overlap on that silence first — an incoming beat with a lead gap ≥ the overlap is not heard to interrupt.

braidio.weave.extract_padded(asset_path, start_s, end_s, out_path, *, pre_roll_s=0.4, post_roll_s=0.3, fade_in_s=0.5, fade_out_s=0.8, min_len_s=2.2)[source]#

Extract [start_s-pre_roll, end_s+post_roll] with in/out fades.

The target words sit in the middle; the padded, faded head/tail are the parts that overlap (tuck under) neighbouring narration in the weave.

Two guards keep short clips from sounding like they just swell in and out (no steady body):

  • the tail is extended so the clip is at least min_len_s long, giving the fade somewhere to breathe;

  • the fades are adaptive — capped to a fraction of the clip so they never swallow it (a 1.5 s clip gets ~0.3 s fades, not 0.5 s + 0.8 s).

The (prev, cur) sequential-part pairs where cur interrupts prev.

The one definition layout_placed() (where to start) and duck_regions() (what to duck) share: overlap is on, both parts are narration (a clip or sting never interrupts nor is interrupted), both have a speaker, and the speakers differ.

braidio.weave.layout_placed(kinds, durs, placements, *, clip_edge_overlap_s, narration_crossfade_s, speakers=None, overlap=None)[source]#

Start offset (s) of each part, placement-aware. Pure function.

Sequential parts advance a running cursor: a clip (or a part following a clip) starts clip_edge_overlap_s before the cursor so its faded edges tuck under the neighbour; narration-after-narration overlaps by narration_crossfade_s. An "under" part starts at the cursor (concurrent with the next sequential part) and does not advance it — so it overlays what follows. Clamped ≥ 0.

With speakers (one id per part, None = no speaker) and an overlap that is on, each boundary interrupting_boundaries() names starts up to max_s early instead (an interruption), capped by both turns’ lengths and shortened by a jitter drawn from Random(f"{seed}:{i}") — per part index, so editing one boundary does not reshuffle the others. Overlap is measured between file boundaries, lead silence included (see TurnOverlap).

While overlap is on, two floors keep it to two voices at once: a part laid after a non-clip part never starts before the end of any earlier sequential part but the last, and a narration part never starts before the end of any earlier narration but the most recent. Neither applies with overlap off, so the default layout is unchanged.

>>> layout_placed(["narration"] * 3, [2.0, 0.3, 2.0], ["sequential"] * 3,
...     clip_edge_overlap_s=0.5, narration_crossfade_s=0.12,
...     speakers=["a", "b", "b"], overlap=TurnOverlap(0.4, 0.0))
[0.0, 1.7, 2.0]

braidio.weave.weave_timeline(items, out_path, *, clip_edge_overlap_s=0.5, narration_crossfade_s=0.12, target_lufs=-16.0, true_peak=-1.0, sample_rate=44100, bed=None, overlap=None)[source]#

Place items on a timeline and mix. Clips overlap neighbours by clip_edge_overlap_s (their faded edges tuck under narration); narration parts butt-join with a small crossfade. Returns out_path.

bed (a MusicBed) lays an instrumental underscore under the whole production: it’s rendered to cover the timeline, attenuated, and mixed in posted by bed.lead_in_s. Items marked spotlight open a gap in it (the bed is rendered as the regions around them — see braidio.music.bed_regions()); with none marked the bed is the single whole-span file. Falls back to a plain concat feel when clip_edge_overlap_s == 0 and there’s nothing to overlay.

overlap (a TurnOverlap, e.g. WeaveConfig.turn_overlap()) lays a change of speaker as an interruption and, with duck_db, lowers the outgoing voice’s talked-over tail (see layout_placed()).
