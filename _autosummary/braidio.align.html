braidio.align#

Time an authored script on narration braidio did not render (braidio#212).

captions_for() and plan_spans() both need the TimelineBreakdown of braidio’s own render. Narration made elsewhere has none: a long script voiced track by track through another TTS, or a recording somebody made by hand. This module builds that timeline from the audio’s measured word times (any word-level transcript of it: ElevenLabs Scribe, Whisper, a forced aligner), while the words that appear in a caption stay the authored ones. Only the times are measured; a recogniser that heard “mate” for «Matte.» never reaches a subtitle.

The one entry point is align_script():

alignment = align_script(script_or_text, words)
alignment.timeline          # plugs into braidio.video.plan_spans
captions_from_cues(alignment.cues)   # the SRT

Free, offline, pure and dependency-free (stdlib difflib): part of the functional core.

Seams (keyword-only, each defaulting to the strongest built-in):

  • normalize: how a text is reduced to comparable tokens (default: lowercase, accents folded, letters and digits of any alphabet).

  • unit: what a timeline beat is, one per "paragraph" or one per "beat".

  • max_chars: the longest caption cue before it is cut at whitespace (the SRT’s line width is captions_from_cues()’s own max_chars).

A dialogue beat is timed as its turns’ text, one unit per turn; the speaker labels are not spoken, so they appear in no cue (unlike cues_for()).

This is the in-package stand-in for a shared word aligner (authored tokens against timed words), a kind of code several packages of the fleet carry; see braidio#212.

Examples

>>> words = [
...     {"text": "Hello", "start": 0.5, "end": 0.9},
...     {"text": "there.", "start": 0.9, "end": 1.4},
...     {"text": "Goodbye", "start": 3.0, "end": 3.4},
... ]
>>> a = align_script("Hello there.\n\nGoodbye.", words, duration_s=4.0)
>>> [(c.text, c.start_s, c.end_s) for c in a.cues]
[('Hello there.', 0.5, 1.4), ('Goodbye.', 3.0, 3.4)]
>>> [(b.start, b.duration) for b in a.timeline.beats]
[(0.0, 3.0), (3.0, 1.0)]
>>> a.unmatched, a.matched, a.total
((), 3, 3)

What align_script() measured.

timeline: one sequential beat per unit, contiguous from 0 to the end of the audio (a beat is its text plus the silence after it, the first also holds the lead-in), so plan_spans and captions take it as they take a render’s. cues: caption cues carrying the authored text at measured times, never overlapping. unmatched: the chunks no heard word matched, whose times were interpolated. matched / total: authored tokens a heard word matched, over all of them: gate on matched / total before trusting the times of audio you are unsure belongs to this text.

braidio.align.align_script(script_or_text, words, *, duration_s=None, unit='paragraph', max_chars=84, normalize=<function tokens>, title='')[source]#

Align the authored script_or_text to the measured words of its audio.

Parameters:

  • script_or_text (Script | str) – a Script (its narration and dialogue beats; segment beats are other audio and are skipped) or plain text, paragraphs separated by a blank line. [tags] and <break/> markup is not spoken and is dropped.

  • words (Iterable[Mapping[str, Any]]) – {"text" (or "word"), "start", "end"} mappings in playback order, times in seconds on the audio’s own clock. Entries whose type is spacing / audio_event are ignored. For audio made in several tracks, offset each track’s words and concatenate.

  • duration_s (float | None) – length of the audio; the last beat runs to it. Default: the end of the last cue.

  • unit (str) – "paragraph" (a timeline beat per paragraph) or "beat" (one per script beat; for plain text, the whole text).

  • max_chars (int) – longest caption cue, cut at whitespace.

  • normalize (Callable[[str], list[str]]) – text to comparable tokens, see tokens().

  • title (str) – the timeline’s title.

Return type:

Alignment

Returns:

An Alignment.

Raises:

ValueError – unit unknown; no text or no usable words; words out of order (an un-offset track) or running past duration_s; or no authored word matched a heard one (wrong audio, or normalize drops everything).
